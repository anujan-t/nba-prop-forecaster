COURTSIDE / NBA PLAYER PROP FORECASTER
===================================

A local research application, not a betting service or financial advice.
Synthetic samples are not real NBA results. No accuracy or profit guarantee.

DIRECT NBA DATA (DEFAULT APPLICATION WORKFLOW)
  The main selector loads NBA's complete current roster from CommonAllPlayers.
  Search by name or team, optionally filter by franchise, and choose any
  player. There is no five-player or 25-result discovery limit. Players do
  not need prior appearances to be listed, and new rookie IDs need not exist
  in nba_api's bundled static directory.
  Profiles, career records and game history load automatically on selection.
  The default Recent window explicitly pools current + previous NBA seasons.
  Choose a single season or Playoffs when that is the intended study.
  No CSV, separate import step or API key is required. Saved history is a
  cache of past studies, not a limit on league coverage.

  The roster is the provider's roster-status=1 snapshot, with a visible
  fetched-at timestamp and a Refresh roster control. Missing franchises or
  ambiguous roster rows produce an error rather than a false full-league
  claim. Roster membership does not mean injury-free or available to play.

  Open Player intel for current team, position, jersey, age, height/weight,
  draft and school/country information; per-game averages and shooting;
  last-5/10/20, season, home/away and opponent splits; complete game logs;
  and regular-season/playoff career records. Full box score exposes shot
  attempts, threes, turnovers, rebounds, fouls, +/- and source identifiers.
  Every returned game is accessible using 25-row pagination.
  Unknown optional data and zero-attempt shooting rates remain unknown,
  not fabricated zeros. Career TOT rows are counted once for traded players.
  Shooting percentages are total makes / total attempts, not mean rates.

  Provider: NBA Stats endpoints at stats.nba.com via the community-maintained
  nba_api client (https://github.com/swar/nba_api). This is not a supported
  NBA commercial API contract. Public endpoints can change, time out or
  block requests. No HTML scraping or access-control bypass is used.

  Responses go directly from the API into validated game records and a
  local SQLite JSON-record cache. The NBA workflow never serializes or
  parses CSV. Loading the same player, history window and game type reuses a
  response fetched within six hours; older responses are fetched again.
  "Refresh player" requests the profile, career and active history again. Refresh
  replaces the same history ID, preserving notebook entries. Failed
  refreshes show an error and leave previously saved data untouched.
  Selecting an already saved history does not contact the API; its actual
  fetched-at time is displayed. No scheduled background sync is implied.

  Completed games only, not live odds or injury reports. At least 10 games
  are needed for a forecast and 16 for walk-forward evaluation. A new
  season or a player with limited appearances may not meet that threshold.
  Such players remain fully selectable, with all available profile and
  game information. No-games and limited-sample states do not manufacture
  forecasts. The app does not request seasons before a listed NBA debut.
  A failed profile, career or game request is reported independently; it
  does not hide other successfully retrieved sections.

DATA AND PRIVACY
  864 seeded synthetic player-games, 12 illustrative named players, five
  count statistics: points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks.
  Seed: 20261027. Player names/teams in this fixture are illustrative, not
  evidence that a player produced these statistics or played for that team.
  NBA history is the primary data source. The service may time out or be
  blocked by NBA Stats. Failures are reported, never silently replaced by
  synthetic data. No live injury/lineup/odds feeds are included.
  The old CSV endpoints and developer CLI remain for backward compatibility
  but CSV controls are removed from the application. Existing saved data is
  preserved and migrated once to native records; the legacy datasets table
  is retained as a backup, not used for ongoing forecasting.
  Legacy CSV imports stay local. Exporting the example CSV and reimporting it does
  not turn it into real data; CSV provenance is always user-supplied.
  Required columns:
    date,player_id,player_name,team,opponent,minutes,points,rebounds,
    assists,steals,blocks
  Optional: home (true/false, blank or omitted means unknown); season,
  game_id, win, field_goals_made/attempted, three_pointers_made/attempted,
  free_throws_made/attempted, offensive_rebounds, defensive_rebounds,
  turnovers, fouls, plus_minus. The NBA endpoint supplies these directly.
  Rest days are derived chronologically.
  Dates must be YYYY-MM-DD. Exclude DNPs (minutes must be positive).
  Limits: 5 MB and 50,000 rows. Duplicate player/date rows are rejected.
  Native game history and snapshots persist in runtime\courtside.sqlite3.
  Override storage with NBA_DATA_DIR. runtime/ is excluded from source control.
  Local-only server; do not expose it publicly without authentication,
  resource limits, monitoring and a production deployment design.

MATHEMATICS
  Empirical: frequency distribution of trailing observed integer totals.
  Normal: discretized at half-count boundaries and truncated below -0.5.
  Poisson: estimated rate equals trailing mean; assumes variance = mean.
  Negative binomial: moment-matched mean and excess variance; uses the
  Poisson limit when excess variance cannot be estimated.
  Auto: normal for points/rebounds/assists. For steals/blocks, selects
  negative binomial if observed variance > 1.5 * mean, otherwise Poisson.
  This is an explicit heuristic, not automated validated model selection.
  Context: minutes scaling plus historical per-minute rates for rest
  (0, 1, 2, 3+ days) and opponent, shrunk with a 12-game prior weight.
  Rest/opponent factors are each capped at +/-20%. They are not causal
  estimates and do not incorporate injuries or confirmed starting lineups.
  Minutes scenarios outside 10%-300% of trailing minutes are rejected.
  Empirical scenario scaling rounds counts to the nearest integer.
  Game outcomes model the player appearing, not DNP/void-settlement rules.

  American odds: positive a -> 100/(a+100); negative a -> |a|/(|a|+100).
  No-vig: divide each implied probability by their two-sided sum.
  Over = P(X > line); under = P(X < line); push = P(X = line).
  On integer lines, compare model and no-vig probabilities conditional on
  no push. All-push model edges are undefined, not zero.
  Expected net return per unit = P(win)*decimal_profit - P(loss).
  A push returns the stake and contributes zero net return.
  Displayed 80% intervals are predictive ranges, not confidence in winning.
  Distribution charts omit at most 0.05% of upper-tail mass.

EVALUATION
  Walk-forward: start after 15 games; train only on older rows. The
  lookback is 10-82 games. No random train/test split or future game leakage.
  Historical target-game minutes are NEVER used as a predictive input.
  Rest and opponent come from the schedule; minutes use the trailing mean.
  A line is either fixed by the user or generated as prior median rounded
  down plus 0.5. Neither mode represents archived sportsbook market lines.
  Pushes are excluded; scoring uses conditional settled probabilities.
  A 1e-6 symmetric prior handles degenerate all-push predictions.
  Brier = mean((probability - outcome)^2); lower is better.
  Baseline is constant 50% (Brier 0.25). Skill = 1 - model_Brier / 0.25.
  Log loss clips probabilities to [1e-6, 1-1e-6].
  Calibration buckets show mean predicted versus observed over frequency
  and sample counts. Sparse buckets should not be over-interpreted.
  Model comparisons are exploratory; selecting a model based on these
  scores needs a separate future holdout before claiming generalization.
  Each backtest is limited to 5,000 evaluation games; select a single
  player or shorter dataset for larger imports.

LINE MOVEMENT
  Manual timestamped snapshots only, separated by dataset/player/stat/game
  date/book. No sportsbook scraping, paid API or automatic wagering.

PARLAY BUILDER
  Combine 1-10 legs (player, stat, line, over/under). Each player+stat pair
  may appear once. Each leg uses exactly the Forecast lab model and settings.
  Linked legs (same player, or players whose latest game was for the same
  team) are joined with a Gaussian copula:
    - Spearman rank correlation over shared games: each player's last 30
      games (or a longer lookback), matched on date and team for teammates.
    - Latent correlation = 2*sin(pi*rho_s/6), shrunk by n/(n+10) toward
      zero, capped at +/-0.95. Fewer than 10 shared games, or a constant
      stat, means no linkage is assumed. Over legs flip the latent sign.
    - The joint win probability is the multivariate normal orthant
      probability (Genz algorithm, fixed seed), bounded by the Frechet
      limits; an invalid correlation matrix is repaired by eigenvalue
      clipping and reported.
  Players on different current teams are treated as independent, even if
  they meet in the same game (head-to-head linkage is not modelled).
  A push counts as not winning. When a whole-number line can push, the
  no-loss probability (every leg wins or pushes) is shown separately, and
  the expected return against entered parlay odds treats a push as a loss.
  The slip also reports how often every linked leg actually won together
  in shared past games, as a check on the model. Fair odds = 1/probability.
  Research only: probabilities, not certainties; no wagering is automated.

              model, lookback, expected_minutes, rest_days, opponent,
              use_context}], book_odds: American odds or null}

  Player history returns ready, limited or empty, plus history metadata,
