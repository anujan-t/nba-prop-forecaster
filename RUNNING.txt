COURTSIDE / NBA PLAYER PROP FORECASTER
===================================

A local research application, not a betting service or financial advice.
Synthetic samples are not real NBA results. No accuracy or profit guarantee.

APP LAYOUT
  Five tabs: Players (search any current player; sub-tabs Props, Game log,
  Profile & career, Lines), Parlay, My Bets, News (Headlines, Injury report,
  Teams & coaches, Roster moves) and Coach. The header menu holds How it
  works (Method, Accuracy) and Settings (Appearance, Model, Data, Coach).
  Pages have links such as #/players, #/parlay, #/bets, #/news/injuries,
  #/news/teams/lal, #/coach, #/how-it-works/accuracy and #/settings/model.

MARKETS (22)
  Core: points, rebounds, assists, 3-pointers made, steals, blocks, turnovers.
  Combos: PRA, pts+reb, pts+ast, reb+ast, steals+blocks ("stocks").
  Shooting: field goals made/attempted, 3-point attempts, free throws
  made/attempted. Also offensive and defensive rebounds and personal fouls.
  Milestones: double-double and triple-double (yes/no; the line is 0.5,
  over = yes). Every market is one integer per game built from box-score
  columns, so forecasts, backtests, parlays, grading and calibration all use
  it. Markets beyond the five basic counts need full box scores (NBA history
  has them; the synthetic fixture and old CSVs do not).

NEWS
  Headlines (classified as injuries and availability, roster moves, coaches
  and front office, games, betting and fantasy, or around the league), the
  league injury report and roster moves come from ESPN's public NBA JSON
  feeds (site.api.espn.com; unofficial, unauthenticated, may change).
  Coaching staffs and team playing styles (record, pace, offensive and
  defensive rating, shot profile, four factors, what each team allows, and
  Synergy offensive play-type frequencies and efficiency, each with its
  league rank) come from NBA Stats via nba_api, for this season or last.
  Feeds are cached: headlines 10 minutes, injuries 15, roster moves 30,
  team styles 6 hours, coaching staffs 24 hours. If a provider fails, the
  last saved copy is shown and marked as saved. Players shows whether the
  selected player is listed and which teammates are out; Props and Parlay
  warn when a leg's player is listed. Forecasts do NOT adjust for injuries
  or styles automatically: they assume the player plays his recent role.

DIRECT NBA DATA (DEFAULT APPLICATION WORKFLOW)
  The main selector loads NBA's complete current roster from CommonAllPlayers.
  Search by name or team, optionally filter by franchise, and choose any
  player. There is no five-player or 25-result discovery limit. Players do
  not need prior appearances to be listed, and new rookie IDs need not exist
  in nba_api's bundled static directory.
  Profiles, career records and game history load automatically on selection.
  The default Recent window explicitly pools current + previous NBA seasons.
  Choose a single season or Playoffs when that is the intended study.
  No CSV, separate import step or API key is required. Saved history is a
  cache of past studies, not a limit on league coverage.

  The roster is the provider's roster-status=1 snapshot, with a visible
  fetched-at timestamp and a Refresh roster control. Missing franchises or
  ambiguous roster rows produce an error rather than a false full-league
  claim. Roster membership does not mean injury-free or available to play.

  A player's Game log and Profile & career sub-tabs show current team, position, jersey, age, height/weight,
  draft and school/country information; per-game averages and shooting;
  last-5/10/20, season, home/away and opponent splits; complete game logs;
  and regular-season/playoff career records. Full box score exposes shot
  attempts, threes, turnovers, rebounds, fouls, +/- and source identifiers.
  Every returned game is accessible using 25-row pagination.
  Unknown optional data and zero-attempt shooting rates remain unknown,
  not fabricated zeros. Career TOT rows are counted once for traded players.
  Shooting percentages are total makes / total attempts, not mean rates.

  Provider: NBA Stats endpoints at stats.nba.com via the community-maintained
  nba_api client (https://github.com/swar/nba_api). This is not a supported
  NBA commercial API contract. Public endpoints can change, time out or
  block requests. No HTML scraping or access-control bypass is used.

  Responses go directly from the API into validated game records and a
  local SQLite JSON-record cache. The NBA workflow never serializes or
  parses CSV. Loading the same player, history window and game type reuses a
  response fetched within six hours; older responses are fetched again.
  "Refresh player" requests the profile, career and active history again. Refresh
  replaces the same history ID, preserving notebook entries. Failed
  refreshes show an error and leave previously saved data untouched.
  Selecting an already saved history does not contact the API; its actual
  fetched-at time is displayed. No scheduled background sync is implied.

  Completed games only, not live odds or injury reports. At least 10 games
  are needed for a forecast and 16 for walk-forward evaluation. A new
  season or a player with limited appearances may not meet that threshold.
  Such players remain fully selectable, with all available profile and
  game information. No-games and limited-sample states do not manufacture
  forecasts. The app does not request seasons before a listed NBA debut.
  A failed profile, career or game request is reported independently; it
  does not hide other successfully retrieved sections.

DATA AND PRIVACY
  864 seeded synthetic player-games, 12 illustrative named players, five
  count statistics: points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks.
  Seed: 20261027. Player names/teams in this fixture are illustrative, not
  evidence that a player produced these statistics or played for that team.
  NBA history is the primary data source. The service may time out or be
  blocked by NBA Stats. Failures are reported, never silently replaced by
  synthetic data. The injury report and headlines in News are read-only
  feeds that the model does not use; there are no lineup or odds feeds.
  The old CSV endpoints and developer CLI remain for backward compatibility
  but CSV controls are removed from the application. Existing saved data is
  preserved and migrated once to native records; the legacy datasets table
  is retained as a backup, not used for ongoing forecasting.
  Legacy CSV imports stay local. Exporting the example CSV and reimporting it does
  not turn it into real data; CSV provenance is always user-supplied.
  Required columns:
    date,player_id,player_name,team,opponent,minutes,points,rebounds,
    assists,steals,blocks
  Optional: home (true/false, blank or omitted means unknown); season,
  game_id, win, field_goals_made/attempted, three_pointers_made/attempted,
  free_throws_made/attempted, offensive_rebounds, defensive_rebounds,
  turnovers, fouls, plus_minus. The NBA endpoint supplies these directly.
  Rest days are derived chronologically.
  Dates must be YYYY-MM-DD. Exclude DNPs (minutes must be positive).
  Limits: 5 MB and 50,000 rows. Duplicate player/date rows are rejected.
  Native game history and snapshots persist in runtime\courtside.sqlite3.
  Override storage with NBA_DATA_DIR. runtime/ is excluded from source control.
  Local-only server; do not expose it publicly without authentication,
  resource limits, monitoring and a production deployment design.

MATHEMATICS
  Game simulator (Auto's choice for every market the box scores support):
    Each forecast plays the player's next game N times (Settings > Model:
    100,000 / 250,000 default / 500,000 / 1,000,000; seeded, so identical
    inputs give identical answers). Per simulated game:
    - minutes are drawn from recent minutes, recency-weighted (10-game
      half-life) and kernel-smoothed; a minutes scenario rescales them;
    - each usage count (2-point and 3-point attempts, free-throw attempts,
      offensive and defensive rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, turnovers,
      fouls) gets a per-minute rate drawn around its recency-weighted
      estimate (gamma, variance = estimation uncertainty), times the minutes;
    - counts are drawn with the dispersion real box scores show at fixed
      minutes (binomial when tighter than Poisson, gamma-Poisson when looser;
      league priors measured on 8,417 player-games, shrunk with 30 games);
    - makes are binomial from attempts at a shooting percentage drawn from
      its Beta posterior (league priors 54% / 36% / 78% with 100 / 150 / 25
      attempts of weight; 30-game half-life); fouls stop at six.
    Points = 2 x made twos + 3 x made threes + made free throws; every market
    is counted from the same simulated games. Half-lives: usage and minutes
    10 games, free-throw attempts and defensive rebounds 12, offensive
    rebounds and turnovers 15, fouls 20, steals and blocks 30. Rest and
    opponent factors (shrunk, capped at +/-20%) scale the usage rates.
    Simulation error of a probability: sqrt(p(1-p)/N), at most 0.10 points
    at 250,000 games. More simulations reduce that noise only; accuracy comes
    from the model and is measured by the walk-forward test below.
  Empirical: frequency distribution of trailing observed integer totals.
  Normal: discretized at half-count boundaries and truncated below -0.5.
  Poisson: estimated rate equals trailing mean; assumes variance = mean.
  Negative binomial: moment-matched mean and excess variance; uses the
  Poisson limit when excess variance cannot be estimated.
  Auto: the game simulator when the market can be built from the box scores
  (all 22 with NBA history; points/rebounds/assists/steals/blocks, their
  combos and milestones with five-count data). Otherwise normal for large
  counts, negative binomial (variance > 1.5 x mean) or Poisson for small
  counts, empirical for yes/no markets.
  Model selection: on 232,288 walk-forward predictions from 8,417 real
  2024-25 and 2025-26 games (70 players, every third game, 60 prior games,
  five lines around each trailing median), the simulator beat the previous
  Auto rule on all 22 markets by log loss (overall 0.5239 -> 0.5150; Brier
  0.1749 -> 0.1718; Brier was level only on double-doubles). Same-player
  pairs: "both win" log loss 0.5104 simulated jointly vs 0.5141 with the
  shared-games correlation and 0.5323 independent (28,464 held-out pairs).
  Context: minutes scaling plus historical per-minute rates for rest
  (0, 1, 2, 3+ days) and opponent, shrunk with a 12-game prior weight.
  Rest/opponent factors are each capped at +/-20%. They are not causal
  estimates and do not incorporate injuries or confirmed starting lineups.
  Minutes scenarios outside 10%-300% of trailing minutes are rejected.
  Empirical scenario scaling rounds counts to the nearest integer.
  Game outcomes model the player appearing, not DNP/void-settlement rules.
  Default lookback: 60 games (recency weights decide how much old games
  count in the simulator).

  American odds: positive a -> 100/(a+100); negative a -> |a|/(|a|+100).
  No-vig: divide each implied probability by their two-sided sum.
  Over = P(X > line); under = P(X < line); push = P(X = line).
  On integer lines, compare model and no-vig probabilities conditional on
  no push. All-push model edges are undefined, not zero.
  Expected net return per unit = P(win)*decimal_profit - P(loss).
  A push returns the stake and contributes zero net return.
  Displayed 80% intervals are predictive ranges, not confidence in winning.
  Distribution charts omit at most 0.05% of upper-tail mass.

EVALUATION
  Walk-forward: start after 15 games; train only on older rows. The
  lookback is 10-82 games. No random train/test split or future game leakage.
  Historical target-game minutes are NEVER used as a predictive input.
  Rest and opponent come from the schedule; minutes use the trailing mean
  (analytic models) or the recent minutes distribution (game simulator).
  A line is either fixed by the user or generated as prior median rounded
  down plus 0.5 (0.5 for yes/no markets). Neither mode represents archived
  sportsbook market lines.
  The game simulator scores each held-out game from 8,000 simulated games
  (simulation error well below the forecast error being measured).
  Pushes are excluded; scoring uses conditional settled probabilities.
  A 1e-6 symmetric prior handles degenerate all-push predictions.
  Brier = mean((probability - outcome)^2); lower is better.
  Baseline is constant 50% (Brier 0.25). Skill = 1 - model_Brier / 0.25.
  Log loss clips probabilities to [1e-6, 1-1e-6].
  Calibration buckets show mean predicted versus observed over frequency
  and sample counts. Sparse buckets should not be over-interpreted.
  Model comparisons are exploratory; selecting a model based on these
  scores needs a separate future holdout before claiming generalization.
  Each backtest is limited to 5,000 evaluation games; select a single
  player or shorter dataset for larger imports.

LINE MOVEMENT
  Manual timestamped snapshots only, separated by dataset/player/stat/game
  date/book. No sportsbook scraping, paid API or automatic wagering.

PARLAY BUILDER
  Combine 1-10 legs (player, market, line, over/under). Each player+market
  pair may appear once. Each leg uses exactly the Props view model and
  settings.
  Same-player legs on the game simulator are priced jointly: the chance
  that they all win is the share of the same simulated games in which they
  all win (exact under the model). Each such pair also reports the latent
  correlation that reproduces its simulated joint chance.
  Other linked legs (same player on an analytic model, or players whose
  latest game was for the same team) are joined with a Gaussian copula:
    - Spearman rank correlation over shared games: each player's last 30
      games (or a longer lookback), matched on date and team for teammates.
    - Latent correlation = 2*sin(pi*rho_s/6), shrunk by n/(n+10) toward
      zero, capped at +/-0.95. Fewer than 10 shared games, or a constant
      stat, means no linkage is assumed. Over legs flip the latent sign.
    - The joint win probability is the multivariate normal orthant
      probability (Genz algorithm, fixed seed), bounded by the Frechet
      limits; an invalid correlation matrix is repaired by eigenvalue
      clipping and reported. Each simulated same-player block is replaced
      by its exact simulated chance (the copula carries links between
      blocks).
  Players on different current teams are treated as independent, even if
  they meet in the same game (head-to-head linkage is not modelled).
  A push counts as not winning. When a whole-number line can push, the
  no-loss probability (every leg wins or pushes) is shown separately, and
  the expected return against entered parlay odds treats a push as a loss.
  The slip also reports how often every linked leg actually won together
  in shared past games, as a check on the model. Fair odds = 1/probability.
  Research only: probabilities, not certainties; no wagering is automated.

COURTSIDE COACH (CHAT)
  A chat panel on every view that answers any NBA betting question about the
  league or the app. It works offline with no setup: a built-in parser
  understands commands and questions and calls the same forecast, parlay,
  history and newsroom code as the UI.
    Build:    "add Jokic over 26.5 points and Murray 21+ points",
              "add Jokic over 45.5 PRA", "Curry over 4.5 threes",
              "Gobert over 2.5 steals + blocks", "add Jokic double-double",
              "remove leg 2", "flip the Murray leg", "change leg 1 line to
              27.5", "make it the under", "undo", "clear my slip"
    Price:    "what are my odds?", "book has it at +450, good price?",
              "Jokic over 26.5 at -115?" (odds: +450, -120, 5.5x, 9/2, evens)
    Research: "what's SGA averaging?", "how often has Wemby gone over 3.5
              blocks in his last 10 games?", "why are my legs linked?",
              "how old is Wemby?", "tell me about Luka"
    League:   "who's out tonight?", "is LeBron playing tonight?", "who's
              injured on the Lakers?", "when is Edwards coming back?",
              "latest news", "Celtics news", "any trades?", "did the Lakers
              cut anyone?", "who coaches the Warriors?", "how do the Thunder
              play?", "how good is the Celtics defense?", "which team plays
              the fastest?", "who gives up the most threes?", "standings",
              "Lakers record", "OKC roster", "Nuggets vs Suns"
    Games:    "what games are on tonight?", "games on Saturday", "when do the
              Lakers play next?", "what's the spread for the Celtics game?",
              "who's favored tonight?", "did the Knicks win last night?",
              "scores"
    The app:  "how does the simulator work?", "how accurate is the model?",
              "how does calibration work?", "where does the data come from?",
              "how do I change the theme?", "what can you do?"
    Betting:  "what does +150 mean?", "convert -135 to decimal", "9/2 in
              american odds", "how much do I win on $20 at +350?", "what's
              the vig?", "what's EV?", "what's a push?", "what's PRA?"
    Track:    "track this for $10 at +450 tonight", "grade my bets"
    Memory:   "remember joker means Nikola Jokic", "forget joker"
  League answers come from the newsroom feeds (cached; stale copies are
  labeled) with cards that open the matching News, How it works or Settings
  page. Forecasts in chat also mention the player's injury status and next
  game. App and betting-term answers come from a built-in knowledge base
  (backend/knowledge.py). Questions about other sports or unrelated topics
  are declined, and the coach never gives picks or "locks".
  Ambiguous names ("Murray", "Tyrese") prompt a choice; the choice is
  remembered. Team names, nicknames, cities and abbreviations are recognized
  ("Sixers", "Dubs", "OKC"; MIN/WAS only in capitals). The chat edits the
  same slip as the UI (undo restores it).
  Follow-ups stay on the player being discussed: after "how about Curry",
  "over or under 23.5?", "what about the under?" or "and 26.5?" ask about
  Curry. A line without a stat means the market whose recent average is
  clearly nearest (said as "Assuming you mean points"); when two markets fit
  about equally, the coach asks which. Short answers to its own questions
  ("points", "22.5", "the under") complete the request it asked about.
  House comparison: break-even = 1 / decimal odds; expected return per unit =
  p * (decimal - 1) - (1 - p); house edge = minus that. Without entered
  odds it assumes -110 on every leg priced as independent, and says so.
  Single props refund pushes, so their comparison is conditional on settling.

  Optional language model: set COURTSIDE_LLM_MODEL (plus
  COURTSIDE_LLM_API_KEY and/or COURTSIDE_LLM_BASE_URL) in a git-ignored
  .env file; see .env.example (OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter, Azure OpenAI
  v1, local Ollama or LM Studio). Slip commands still run through the
  built-in parser; every other message goes to the model, which can only get
  facts by calling the app's tools (forecasts, slip edits and pricing, track
  record, news, injuries, roster moves, games and lines, team profiles,
  coaching staffs, rankings, standings, rosters, player bios, app help, model
  accuracy and odds math). Any failure falls back to the built-in coach for
  that message. Keys are never logged or returned.

GETTING SMARTER OVER TIME
  1. My Bets: tracked bets are graded from NBA box scores the day after
     their game date (won/lost/push; void if the player has no game that
     date). ROI, legs won vs expected and Brier score are reported.
  2. Calibration, separately for every market: walk-forward predictions
     from Auto on every stored player history (several lines around each
     rolling median; 0.5 for yes/no markets) plus graded legs. A shared
     logistic recalibration (logit q = a + b logit p, ridge-shrunk to the
     identity) is fitted on the older three quarters (by date) and tested on
     the newest quarter. Each market then fits its own correction, shrunk
     toward the shared one, and keeps whichever of its own, the shared one or
     the raw model scored best on its share of the newest quarter (better
     log loss by > 0.001 and no worse Brier); markets with fewer than 100
     recent and 100 older predictions use the shared correction when it is
     on. Calibrated probabilities appear beside the raw ones for Auto
     forecasts, chat answers and parlays. It refits in the background when
     new games are imported or bets are graded; a calibration learned on an
     older model version is never applied (it is rebuilt).
  3. Memory: learned nicknames, and thumbs up/down on replies; liked replies
     become examples for the language model.
